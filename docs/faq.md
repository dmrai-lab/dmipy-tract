# dmipy-tract FAQ

## Tracking Algorithms

### 1. Why is RK4 used for deterministic tracking instead of Euler?

4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) reduces the local truncation error of each step from O(h^2) (Euler) to O(h^5), where h is the step size. In practice this means that for the same step size of 0.5 mm, RK4 follows curved fibre pathways far more accurately than Euler integration, which tends to drift off-axis in high-curvature regions such as the u-fibres of the cingulum or the pons crossing fibres. RK4 evaluates the orientation field at four intermediate positions per step (k1 at the current position, k2 and k3 at half-step offsets, k4 at a full-step offset) and combines them as `(k1 + 2k2 + 2k3 + k4) / 6` to obtain a higher-order estimate of the true integral. The extra three field evaluations per step have minimal runtime cost compared to the reduction in trajectory error, and the lower error accumulation means fewer spurious stoppings due to angular-change limits being exceeded. Stochastic tracking, which deliberately introduces angular noise at each step, uses simpler Euler integration because the noise already dominates the error budget.

### 2. What was the sign convention bug in the RK4 implementation, and what symptom did it produce?

In bidirectional RK4 propagation, each half-streamline is grown with `sign = +1.0` (forward) or `sign = -1.0` (backward). An early prototype applied `sign` to the k-vectors themselves: `k2 = self._get_direction(pos + 0.5 * step_size * (sign * k1), sign * k1)`. This meant that when computing the backward half, the reference direction passed into `_get_direction` was negated. The polarity-alignment flip inside `_get_direction` — which flips the retrieved peak if `dot(peak, prev_dir) < 0` — then triggered on the wrong hemisphere, because a negated reference direction looks like it points 180 degrees away from the fibre. The sub-step k-vectors therefore oscillated in sign between evaluations, their weighted sum `(k1 + 2k2 + 2k3 + k4) / 6` tended toward zero, and the backward half produced either a single-point or zero-length streamline that was discarded. The visible symptom was that all streamlines appeared to propagate in only one direction from each seed, giving a tractogram with roughly half the expected streamline length.

### 3. Why is `sign` applied only to position offsets and not to the k-vectors in the RK4 step?

The k-vectors (k1, k2, k3, k4) are unit orientation vectors aligned with the fibre direction. They encode "which way does the fibre run at this point in space?" and by construction are always expressed as unsigned directions, with the polarity set relative to the previous step direction. The `sign` variable encodes "which physical direction should the tracker step in?" — forward or backward along the fibre axis. These two concerns must be separated: the position offset `pos + sign * 0.5 * step * k1` moves toward the correct sub-step sampling location, but `k1` itself is passed to the next `_get_direction` call as the polarity reference without modification. Multiplying `k1` by `sign` before passing it as a reference would invert the hemisphere check and cause the flip logic to activate when it should not, breaking directional consistency across sub-steps. The correct pattern is: k-vectors are always unsigned and used as polarity anchors; `sign` appears only in the scalar multiplier on position offsets and in the final `pos = pos + sign * step_size * step_dir` update.

### 4. When should stochastic tracking be preferred over deterministic tracking?

Stochastic tracking is preferred when the orientation field is noisy or ambiguous, such as in crossing-fibre regions where a single deterministic peak may not represent the true dominant direction at all noise realisations. It is also preferred when downstream analysis (COMMIT, Bayesian connectivity estimation, or uncertainty-aware tractometry) requires a distribution of plausible paths rather than a single best-guess path per seed. `StochasticTracker` generates `n_streamlines_per_seed` (default 3) independent samples per seed by drawing each step direction from a von Mises-Fisher distribution centred on the peak, so the resulting tractogram implicitly encodes orientation uncertainty. The trade-off is that the tractogram is larger (more streamlines per seed) and requires SIFT2 weighting rather than SIFT filtering to correctly account for the varying density. Deterministic tracking is preferred for quality control, visual inspection, and atlas-based bundle segmentation where reproducibility matters more than distributional coverage.

### 5. What does the vMF concentration parameter kappa control, and what are typical values for tractography?

Kappa is the concentration parameter of the von Mises-Fisher distribution used to perturb the step direction in `StochasticTracker`. When kappa is large, the distribution is tightly concentrated around the mean direction (the principal peak), and the drawn step direction is nearly identical to the deterministic direction; as kappa approaches infinity the tracker becomes fully deterministic. When kappa is small (approaching zero), the distribution becomes uniform over the hemisphere, and each step direction is essentially random. The standard deviation of the angular perturbation is approximately `arctan(1 / sqrt(kappa))` radians. Typical values for tractography range from 30 to 100: kappa=50 (the default) gives an angular standard deviation of about 8 degrees, which is close to the typical peak uncertainty from dMRI data at clinical SNR. Values below 10 produce wide dispersion that may defeat the curvature constraint, while values above 200 produce results nearly indistinguishable from deterministic RK4 tracking and should be replaced with `DeterministicTracker` directly.

## SIFT and Filtering

### 6. What is SIFT, and what physical quantity does it try to make the tractogram match?

SIFT (Spherical-deconvolution Informed Filtering of Tractograms; Smith et al. 2013, NeuroImage 67:298-312) is a post-hoc tractogram filtering step that removes streamlines to bring the Track Density Image (TDI) into agreement with a target density map derived from the fitted microstructure model. The physical quantity being matched is the intra-axonal fibre density per voxel, expressed as the intra-cellular volume fraction (vf_ic) times the voxel volume divided by the mean streamline length. Raw tractograms systematically over-represent long, straight fibres (which accumulate more density per voxel per streamline) and under-represent short, curved fibres; SIFT corrects this bias by selectively removing the over-represented streamlines. In dmipy-tract, SIFT uses the biophysical model's `partial_volume_0` parameter map to define the target rather than CSD FOD amplitude, making the filtering directly consistent with the same model used for voxel-level analysis. After SIFT, the tractogram's streamline density is quantitatively proportional to the physical fibre density estimated by the model.

### 7. What is the SIFT cost function, and how does the greedy algorithm minimise it?

The cost function is `C = sum_v (TDI_v - target_density_v)^2`, the sum of squared differences between the current Track Density Image and the target density map over all voxels v. The greedy algorithm in `SIFTFilter.filter()` initialises the TDI as the contribution of all streamlines, then iterates: at each iteration it evaluates the tentative cost of removing each active streamline by temporarily subtracting that streamline's voxel contributions from the current TDI and computing the resulting cost. The streamline whose removal produces the greatest cost reduction (`best_gain > 0`) is permanently removed, the TDI is updated, and the loop repeats. The loop terminates when no single streamline removal reduces the cost, at which point no further greedy improvement is possible. This greedy approach does not guarantee the global optimum (that would require combinatorial search), but in practice it converges to a tractogram with substantially improved density matching. The time complexity is O(N^2) in the number of streamlines, so for large tractograms (>100k streamlines) SIFT2 weights should be used instead.

### 8. What is SIFT2, and when should per-streamline weights be used instead of filtering?

SIFT2 is the weighted variant of SIFT that assigns a non-negative scalar weight w_i to each streamline such that `sum_i w_i * contribution_i(v) ≈ target_density(v)` for all voxels, rather than removing streamlines outright. `SIFTFilter.weights()` builds a dense matrix A of shape `(n_voxels, n_streamlines)` where `A[v, i]` is the length contribution of streamline i to voxel v, then solves the non-negative least squares (NNLS) problem `min ||Aw - b||^2` subject to `w >= 0`. SIFT2 should be preferred over SIFT when stochastic tracking has been used (multiple streamlines per seed encode orientation uncertainty and discarding any of them loses that information), when downstream connectivity analysis needs a continuous weight rather than a binary include/exclude decision, or when tractometric averaging benefits from SIFT2 weights as importance weights. SIFT (greedy removal) is preferable when a compact tractogram with fewer streamlines is required for storage or visualisation, or when the downstream tool cannot handle weighted streamlines.

### 9. How is the target_density for SIFT derived from a dmipy-core fitted model in practice?

The target density map is constructed from the fitted model's intra-cellular volume fraction map `vf_ic` (accessed as `field.parameter_at('partial_volume_0', ...)` across the volume), the voxel volume in mm^3 (product of the three voxel side lengths from the affine), and the mean streamline length in mm. The formula is `target_density_v = vf_ic_v * voxel_volume / mean_streamline_length`, which gives units of dimensionless density (mm/mm^3 * mm = dimensionless) matching the TDI's length-weighted units. In practice you compute the voxel volume as `abs(det(affine[:3,:3]))`, compute the mean streamline length as the mean of `[sum(norms(diff(sl))) for sl in streamlines]`, retrieve `vf_ic` as a 3-D array via `field._params['partial_volume_0']`, and multiply element-wise. The resulting map is passed directly as the `target_density` argument to `SIFTFilter.filter()` or `SIFTFilter.weights()`. This derivation ensures that the SIFT target is physically grounded in the same biophysical model used for stopping and seeding decisions.

## Data Structures and I/O

### 10. What is a Track Density Image (TDI), and what are its units?

A Track Density Image (Calamante et al. 2010, NeuroImage 53:1233-1243) is a 3-D volumetric image where each voxel contains the total length of streamline segments passing through it, accumulated across all streamlines in the tractogram. `compute_tdi` in `dmipy_tract/filtering/tdi.py` sub-samples each streamline segment along its length at twice voxel resolution, rounds each sample point to the nearest voxel, and adds `segment_length_mm / n_samples` to that voxel's accumulator. The units of the result are mm of fibre length per voxel (mm), which when divided by the voxel volume (mm^3) gives fibre length density (mm/mm^3 = mm^-2). TDI is the primary quantity that SIFT tries to match to the model-derived target density. High-resolution TDI maps (computed by upsampling the affine to sub-millimetre resolution) can also serve as a structural connectivity proxy for atlas registration, surpassing conventional FA maps in anatomical contrast in deep white matter structures.

### 11. How does MicrostructureField connect to a dmipy-core FittedMultiCompartmentModel?

`MicrostructureField` is constructed via `MicrostructureField.from_fitted_model(fitted_model, affine)`, which stores the fitted model and the 4x4 voxel-to-world affine, then pre-caches the model's peak orientations as a `(X, Y, Z, K, 3)` float32 array (`fitted_model.peaks_cartesian()`) and all scalar parameter maps as a dictionary of float32 arrays. The affine and its inverse are stored for world-to-voxel coordinate conversion. At query time, `peaks_at(xyz)` converts the world-space position to fractional voxel coordinates using the inverse affine, then performs trilinear interpolation (`scipy.ndimage.map_coordinates` with `order=1`) on each component of each peak, and re-normalises the result to unit length. Similarly, `parameter_at(name, xyz)` interpolates any scalar parameter map at the query location. This design means the tracker operates entirely in world coordinates (mm, RASMM space) and never needs to reason about voxel indices during propagation.

### 12. What does peaks_at() return, and which index is the principal direction?

`MicrostructureField.peaks_at(xyz)` returns an `ndarray` of shape `(K, 3)` where K is the number of orientation peaks in the fitted model (typically 1 for single-fibre NODDI, up to 3 for multi-tensor models). Each row is a trilinearly interpolated unit vector giving the orientation of the k-th fibre population at the world-space position xyz. **Index 0 is always the principal (dominant) direction**; both `DeterministicTracker` and `StochasticTracker` use `peaks[0]` exclusively for propagation. Peaks are L2-normalised by `peaks_at()` before returning, so each row has unit length unless the underlying model returned an all-zero orientation vector at that location (indicating the model did not converge), in which case the trackers break the loop via an explicit `if np.all(peaks[0] == 0): break` guard. Requesting peaks outside the field of view returns the nearest-boundary value due to `mode='nearest'` in `map_coordinates`.

### 13. Why does bbox_valid_check=False need to be passed on both save and load of .trk files?

dipy's `save_tractogram` and `load_tractogram` both perform bounding-box validation: they check that every streamline point lies within the spatial extent defined by the reference NIfTI header's shape and affine. dmipy-tract stores streamlines in world-space RASMM coordinates; when a real reference NIfTI is unavailable a minimal 1x1x1 synthetic header is created, whose bounding box is just one voxel. Any streamline extending beyond 1 voxel in any direction then triggers the validation error `ValueError: The file ... is not valid` on save, and the same check is repeated on load, so a file saved with `bbox_valid_check=False` will still fail on load unless the flag is passed again. The coordinates themselves are geometrically correct; the validation failure is an artifact of the mismatch between the minimal reference header and the actual brain-sized streamlines. Both `save_trk` and `load_trk` in `tractogram_io.py` therefore unconditionally pass `bbox_valid_check=False`.

### 14. What is the Nifti1Header dim bug, and how is it avoided when building a minimal reference image?

nibabel's `Nifti1Header.set_zooms()` reads the `dim[0]` field (number of dimensions) to know how many zoom values to set. When a `Nifti1Header` is constructed directly from `nib.Nifti1Header()` without attached data, `dim[0]` is zero and `set_zooms` raises an `AttributeError` or silently writes pixel dimensions into the wrong slots. The fix is to always construct a `Nifti1Image` first (passing a numpy data array and the affine), then call `set_zooms` on the image's header: `ref = nib.Nifti1Image(np.zeros((1,1,1), dtype=np.uint8), affine); ref.header.set_zooms(voxel_size)`. When `Nifti1Image` is initialised with a data array it populates `dim[0]` correctly (3 for a 3-D array), after which `set_zooms` writes the voxel sizes into the correct `pixdim` slots. In `save_trk`, this synthetic reference image is used only when no real NIfTI reference is supplied; the preferred path is to pass a real NIfTI image so that all header metadata is copied faithfully.

## Integration with dmipy-core

### 15. How does along_tract_profile work, and what microstructure parameters are meaningful to profile?

`along_tract_profile(streamlines, field, parameter_name, n_points=100)` resamples each streamline to 100 equally-spaced arc-length positions (normalised to [0, 1]), queries `field.parameter_at(parameter_name, pt)` at each position, and accumulates the profiles into a `(N_streamlines, n_points)` matrix. It then computes and returns the bundle-mean and standard-deviation profile across all streamlines as `(n_points,)` arrays, optionally weighted by per-streamline SIFT2 weights. Any scalar parameter from the fitted model is meaningful to profile, but the most interpretable are: `partial_volume_0` (NDI / intra-cellular volume fraction, a proxy for axon density), `SD1WatsonDistributed_1_odi` (ODI, orientation dispersion — increases in lesions and partial-volume regions), and compartment diffusivities (sensitive to membrane permeability and axon diameter changes). Arc-length normalisation aligns streamlines of different physical lengths so that position 0.0 is the start and 1.0 is the end; comparing profiles across subjects therefore requires that all streamlines in the bundle run in a consistent anatomical direction (head-to-tail), which bundle segmentation tools can enforce.

### 16. What is the BIDS derivatives directory structure used by save_bids_tractogram?

`save_bids_tractogram` follows the BIDS Derivatives specification, writing outputs to `<output_dir>/<subject>/[<session>/]dwi/` with filenames assembled from BIDS key-value entities: `<subject>[_<session>]_desc-<desc>_<suffix>.trk` and a matching `.json` sidecar. For example, without a session: `derivatives/dmipy-tract/sub-01/dwi/sub-01_desc-wholebrain_tractography.trk`; with a session: `derivatives/dmipy-tract/sub-01/ses-01/dwi/sub-01_ses-01_desc-wholebrain_tractography.trk`. The JSON sidecar records `n_streamlines`, the 4x4 `affine`, `TrackerSoftware`, and a `GeneratedBy` object with the package name and version. The `desc` entity allows multiple tractograms per subject (e.g. `desc-wholebrain`, `desc-cst-left`, `desc-filtered`) without filename collision. The output directory is created with `parents=True, exist_ok=True` so that the full path is always valid on first write.

## Strategy

### 17. Why does dmipy-tract own tractography rather than wrapping MRtrix3 or dipy?

The core motivation is tight biophysical coupling at every stage of the tractography pipeline. MRtrix3's tractography takes CSD fibre orientation distributions as input and has no mechanism to accept intra-cellular volume fraction, ODI, or axon diameter as first-class inputs to stopping criteria or SIFT target density. Wrapping MRtrix3 would require writing parameter maps to disk, converting them to a format MRtrix3 accepts, and then re-reading results, which introduces file-format round-trips and prevents the tracker from querying the fitted model at every step in real time. dipy provides basic tractography with FA-based stopping but similarly lacks biophysical model integration. By owning the implementation, dmipy-tract can: (1) pass `VfIcThreshold` and `OdiThreshold` directly to the propagation loop with no file I/O; (2) derive SIFT target density from the same `FittedMultiCompartmentModel` object used for signal fitting; (3) validate tractography against dmipy-sim Monte Carlo simulations using the same physical ground truth; and (4) perform tractometry using the exact same parameter maps that appear in voxel-level group analyses.

### 18. How are fury imports handled to keep dmipy-tract importable in headless/CI environments?

fury is an optional dependency used only by `plot_tractogram` in `dmipy_tract/vis/tractogram_viewer.py`. The import is deferred to inside the function body using a try/except block: `try: import fury.actor as actor; import fury.window as window; except ImportError as exc: raise ImportError("fury is required...") from exc`. This means that `import dmipy_tract` and all non-visualisation code paths work without fury installed — typical on headless servers, Docker containers, and CI runners. When fury is present but a display is unavailable, `fury.window.record` uses OSMesa off-screen rendering and writes a PNG file when `output_file` is specified; the interactive `window.show` path is not reached in CI. In unit tests, fury is mocked at the module level by inserting `MagicMock()` instances into `sys.modules` before importing the viewer, which allows tests to verify the code path and argument handling without requiring a real fury installation.

### 19. How does seeds_from_vf_ic differ from seeds_from_mask, and when is each appropriate?

`seeds_from_mask(mask, affine, density)` accepts an arbitrary binary mask and places seeds at voxel centres (or on a sub-voxel grid for `density > 1`) wherever `mask` is True. It makes no assumption about what the mask represents and is appropriate when you have an externally defined seed region (e.g. a hand-drawn ROI, a atlas-derived WM mask, or a tissue-probability thresholded map). `seeds_from_vf_ic(field, vf_ic_threshold, density)` internally constructs a mask from the fitted model's `partial_volume_0` parameter map by thresholding at `vf_ic_threshold` (default 0.3), then calls `seeds_from_mask`. The difference is that `seeds_from_vf_ic` directly ties seeding density to the biophysical model's estimate of axon content: regions with high intra-cellular volume fraction receive more seeds, producing tractograms whose density distribution is biologically informed rather than uniform over a WM mask. Use `seeds_from_vf_ic` for whole-brain tractography where you want seeding to reflect tissue microstructure; use `seeds_from_mask` when the seed region is predetermined by anatomy or experimental design.

### 20. What stopping criteria does default_stopping() combine, and what biophysical quantities do they use?

`default_stopping()` returns a `CompositeStopping` that combines three criteria with logical OR (stop if any criterion triggers). First, `BoundaryStop` halts tracking when the streamline position leaves the imaging field of view (fractional voxel coordinates fall outside `[0, shape-1]`), preventing out-of-bounds field queries. Second, `VfIcThreshold` (default threshold 0.1) stops tracking when the intra-cellular volume fraction parameter `partial_volume_0` falls below 0.1, which corresponds to leaving white matter and entering grey matter, CSF, or pathological tissue where meaningful fibre orientation cannot be estimated. Third, `OdiThreshold` (default threshold 0.8) stops tracking when the orientation dispersion index `SD1WatsonDistributed_1_odi` exceeds 0.8, which indicates near-isotropic orientation distribution (ODI close to 1.0 = isotropic) where the dominant peak direction is undefined. Together these three criteria reflect tissue microstructure rather than the FA or CSD-FOD-amplitude thresholds used by conventional tractography tools, making stopping decisions directly interpretable in terms of biophysical tissue state. Custom thresholds and parameter names can be passed to `default_stopping()` or individual criterion constructors to adapt to different model types.
